### What to Watch For

- Empathy maps with all positive quotes — the "Thinks" quadrant should have fears and uncertainties
- Wireframes designed for IT's needs, not the employee's — check every decision against the empathy map
- Screens that are trying to do too many things

### Facilitation Tips

- Run the swap exercise: swap wireframes between pairs. Ask "What did the empathy map say about this?" for each friction point
- For Thinks quadrant: "What does the employee worry about that they wouldn't say out loud?"
- At debrief: which wireframe decision changed the most after seeing the empathy map? That's the learning.

### Extension / Coaching Office Hours

Participants can continue extension work independently and bring it to **Coaching Office Hours**.
At Office Hours, focus on: what decision did they make, why, and what would they change?

### Your Starting State

You have:
- `research/employee-interviews.md` — 4 employee interview quotes about their IT request experience
- `reference/existing-portal-notes.md` — notes on the current portal that employees avoid

Your goal: build an empathy map, sketch wireframes, then critique them through the empathy map lens.

### Step by Step

**Step 1:** Build the empathy map: Says, Thinks, Does, Feels. Extract 3 key insights.

**Step 2:** Sketch 3 key screens: catalog homepage, service detail, request form.

**Step 3:** Swap wireframes with another participant. Use empathy map insights as your lens: would this user understand this screen?

### What Good Looks Like

An empathy map where "Thinks" reveals something surprising. Wireframes where every design decision can be traced to a user insight.

See the `solutions/` directory for reference examples — but try the exercise first.

### Extension Work

- Run a 5-minute hallway test: ask one person to think aloud while finding a specific service
- Map the IT admin's experience — where do their needs conflict with the employee's?
- Write 3 usability test tasks for your catalog homepage

Bring your extension work to **Coaching Office Hours**. You'll get 15 minutes of focused feedback.
